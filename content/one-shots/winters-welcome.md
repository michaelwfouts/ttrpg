## 1. Adventure Overview

### Premise

Every year, the town of Helvetia holds Fasnacht, a festival of masks, music, and dancing meant to drive off winter and welcome spring. Every year, it works. This year, it doesn't. As the closing song ends, the cold turns *wrong*, the hall freezes solid around the dancers, and the player characters, untouched by whatever has happened, find themselves alone in a room full of the frozen living, with a hole torn in the world where the mountain remembers every winter that never wanted to end.

Old Man Winter did not leave when he was supposed to. Someone asked him to stay.

### What's Really Going On

*For the GM only.*

- **Tonight, someone asked him to stay.** Every spring, the mine lays men off and Helvetia's young people leave. Reverend Cutlip has decided a frozen town is better than an empty one. It is 1930, just after the Wall Street Crash, and the coal mine is one of the town's only steady sources of income, with demand driven by the cold. Alone at his church, at the hour the town sang, he sang the *Litany of the Long Winter*, an invitation for the cold to stay a little longer. Winter accepted. The hall froze in the silence where the final verse should have been.
- **The town's spirits are lost.** The frozen townsfolk's bodies stand in the hall. Their spirits have been pulled loose and wander the **Hollow Season**, a frozen dream-Helvetia built from the memory of winters past, caught in moments that repeat on a loop.
- **The Mask-Maker is trying to fix it.** He is the town's quiet protector, the latest in a long line of keepers. He stands the effigy in the middle of the hall, turns every frozen townsperson to look at it, and means to sing Winter into it, so that Winter has a face that can be sent off.
- **The party decides how it ends.** The party can visit up to three paths (the church, the mine, the old tree). Each yields a **key** that makes one part of the climax easier. When the Mask-Maker finishes, Winter takes the body, and the party has to bring him down. Along the way they can keep the customs of a **wake**, seeing him off the way you'd see off a guest, and each custom stalls or weakens him. At the end he leaves his body behind, and the town only wakes when that body is **burned** on the Fasnacht bonfire.

### Adventure at a Glance

1. **Act One: The Fasnacht Ball** (Section 3). Parade, masks, the hall, the song, and the Freeze. The Mask-Maker steps forward.
2. **Act Two: The Hollow Season** (Section 4). The party steps out into frozen dream-Helvetia: lost souls, masked sprites, and three glowing landmarks.
3. **The Paths** (Sections 5-7). Any number, any order:
   - **Path A: The Watchers in the Nave.** Confront Reverend Cutlip. *Key: the True Final Verse.*
   - **Path B: What Sleeps in the Seam.** Bargain with the Banked Fire. *Key: the Everburning Ember.*
   - **Path C: The Sap Still Running.** Wake the old tree. *Key: the Sarvis Berry.*
4. **Act Three: The Climax** (Section 8). The Turning of the Faces, then the fight, then the Burning.

### Running the Adventure

This is a single-session (~3 hour) one-shot. The party is **free to visit one, two, or all three paths, in any order**. No key is required to finish. The constraint is time: the Mask-Maker's ritual advances as the party works, and each townsperson he turns brings the frozen town closer to stopping for good. Let the party's time, energy, and interest decide how many paths they pursue.

### Tone

This is Old Gods of Appalachia. The horror is generational and tied to grief, tradition, and the mountain's memory, not jump-scares. Old Man Winter is not a villain to be "defeated" so much as a season that has overstayed an invitation. Even in the combat-forward moments, frame things as a bargain, a rite, or a debt, not a boss fight. Let the frozen townsfolk, recognizable mid-laugh, mid-step, mid-drink, be the emotional stakes throughout.

### The Unanswered Mystery

The adventure never explains why the party alone was spared by the freeze. It is a mystery for the table to wonder about, not a puzzle to solve. Don't answer it, and don't let NPCs answer it.

## 2. Key Figures

These are the figures that drive the story. Minor NPCs specific to a single path are detailed in that path's section.

### Old Man Winter

Old Man Winter is not a monster in a mask. He is what the mask is *of*: a season given just enough shape to be spoken to, bargained with, sung at, and, most years, sent away. He has always come. He has always gone. He is not evil, any more than a flood or a fever is evil, but he is old, and hungry in the way that cold is hungry, and every year he leaves a little more reluctantly than the last. This year, someone asked him to stay, and he found he wanted to.

He does not want to destroy Helvetia. He wants to *keep* it: frozen, quiet, safe from spring's habit of taking things away (the layoffs on the first of March, the young folks walking out down the road, the winter's dead who have to be put in the ground once it thaws, the grief that only sharpens once the ice stops numbing it). There is a real, sad logic to him that a thoughtful table may find sympathetic even as they oppose him.

He has no body until summoned into the effigy (see Section 8). Until then he is felt, not seen: in the cold, in the eyes of the effigy, in the Frost Wisps and the Frost Stag.

### The Mask-Maker, the Latest Keeper

*Level 6 for Intellect defense, hiding his intent, and anything to do with the ritual; Level 4 for physical tasks*

No one in Helvetia can quite say how he makes the masks, only that a man appear every year in time for Fasnacht, finished by hands nobody has seen at work. The Mask-Maker never joins in at the Ball. He stands nearby, bare-faced, keeping watch to make sure nothing goes awry.

He is a mortal man who took up the office six years ago, the spring his mentor died. The office is older than the town. It has passed from keeper to apprentice for generations, and each keeper has written what they learned in a stack of journals kept in the workshop. **He is Helvetia's protector.** Every Fasnacht he quietly makes sure that Old Man Winter is given a face, summoned, and sent off. In each of his six years, the rite has held. Tonight it failed, and he did not see it coming and did not cause it.

He is alarmed and, being proud, hides it as irritation. He never asks the party for help. He grumbles, he complains, and he leaves things where they might be found. He cares about the town more than he lets on, and the party can tell.

**What the office gives him.** He is a man, but the office has made him a little more than one:

- He walks the Hollow Season, the church, the mine, and the tree line freely, and nothing there can harm him.
- Masks he works on fit their wearers a little too well.
- His satchel holds more masks than it should.
- The old keepers' words come easily to him, sometimes in voices that aren't quite his own.

```creature cypher
The Mask-Maker
Level 6 for Intellect defense, hiding intent, and the ritual; Level 4 for physical tasks
Health: 20 | Armor: 1

> **The Keeper's Office.** Walks the Hollow Season, the church, the mine, and the tree line freely; nothing there can harm him.

- **Damage.** 4 points (he rarely resorts to this).
```

**Combat.** He does not lift a hand against the party unless they threaten the ritual, and even then he tries to talk first.

**Interaction.** Dry, weary, and always a step ahead. Speaks in half-answers and complaints that are almost directions. He is the one NPC present in every act of the adventure. He treats the party like an unexpected complication in a night that was supposed to be routine.

**GM Intrusion (A Helping Hand):** At a critical moment, the Mask-Maker helps: a hand on a collar, a word at the right time, a stone kicked loose just so. The help is real, and he makes sure the PC who received it knows it.

#### What He's Doing: The Turning of the Faces

**The yearly rite:** Old Man Winter is a season, not a body. Every year the Mask-Maker makes sure the town gives him a "face": the effigy is hung where every masked face in the hall can see it, the town sings to it so the spirit can embody it, and then it is burned, and that is how he is sent off. A face is only a face when someone is looking at it. The town thinks the masks are a custom. They are a safeguard each keeper has built on, one mask at a time, over generations.

**What went wrong:** He doesn't know. Winter would not take the effigy's face, and the freeze came down unexpectedly. New to the job, he hasn't committed the journals to memory, and he is working from half-remembered lessons given by his predecessor. (The GM knows the answer: Reverend Cutlip's invitation. See his entry below.)

#### The Journals

He reads from his predecessors' journals all night, muttering lines aloud, flipping back and forth, and arguing with dead keepers' handwriting. This is how the party gets hints without being asked for help. Sample entries:

- *"Every face needs to see winter in. It's rude to not say hello to those who come knocking at your door."* (The town's gaze comes first.)
- *"Winter is a guest. Treat him as one."* (A clue to the Wake Customs.)
- *"Burn him on the Fasnacht fire. Do not look back."* (The Burning.)
- A margin note in a different hand: *"He did not like the salt."*

Some pages are water-stained or torn, and a few keepers contradict each other. A PC who reads over his shoulder may spot something he missed. He still won't ask for help.

### Reverend Asa Cutlip, the One Who Asked

*Level 5*

Reverend Asa Cutlip is fifty-some years old, gaunt, and soft-spoken, and he is utterly convinced he is doing the most loving thing he has ever done. Due to his quiet nature, his narcissistic delusions are not widely known among the community, but those who see his face on a daily basis are aware. He treats some of the old gods of these mountains as avatars that God has sent into the world. (The GM can use this to tie in other storylines.) He is the antagonist of Path A (Section 5), and the reason this Fasnacht went wrong.

**Spring empties Helvetia.** The town lives on coal considering the state of the economy. The company's working mine, No. 6 up on the ridge, runs hard all winter, when every stove from here to Pittsburgh is burning and coal fetches its best price. Every spring, as the prices fall, the company posts layoffs on the first of March and cuts the shifts back to almost nothing. Then the leaving starts. Young men walk out at the thaw to the steel mills in Ohio, the glass works in Clarksburg, the rail yards anywhere, and whole families follow them. Some come back in the fall. Fewer every year.

The Cutlips have preached in Helvetia since the mine came. Asa's father, Reverend Josiah Cutlip, led the church through the influenza winter of 1918, and Asa has buried this town's dead and blessed its leaving for most of his life.

**His son.** Six years ago, at the thaw, Asa's son Caleb, eighteen and laid off from No. 6, packed a bag for a job in an Ohio mill. Left Fork Creek was running high with snowmelt, the ford was bad, and Caleb didn't wait for it to drop. They found him two miles downstream. Asa has never been able to separate the two things: spring didn't just drown his boy, it *sent him away*, the same as it sends everyone away.

He stopped going to Fasnacht. He couldn't stand in that hall and sing winter off. Then, going through his father's papers, he found the *Litany of the Long Winter*, written by his father's congregation in 1918 to keep their dead with them a little longer. (The GM can tie where the Litany came from to other gods of the setting.) Asa saw a way to keep the living. Tonight, at the hour the town sang, he sang the Litany alone in his church, and something in the mountain answered.

**What he believes.** A winter that never ends means No. 6 never slows and never closes. Nobody's laid off, nobody walks the ford, and every family stays at one table. He knows exactly what it costs: a town held still, frozen, unable to grow or change. He has decided that a frozen Helvetia is better than an empty one. And he isn't entirely wrong. Spring really does empty this town, and the party has just seen it at its fullest.

He was never in the hall, so he was never frozen. He walked into the Hollow Season on his own feet, and he waits for the party at the church altar, leading the hymn. He does not think of himself as fighting the town. He thinks of himself as the only one who has finally, finally decided to save it.

```creature cypher
Reverend Asa Cutlip
Level 5
Health: 20 | Armor: 0

> **Unafraid.** Interaction tasks to intimidate or threaten him are hindered by one step.
> **Grieving Father.** Interaction tasks that genuinely engage with what he lost, or with the town's real hardship, are eased by one step.

- **Damage.** 3 points (clumsy, almost reluctant violence).
- **Borrowed Cold (Once per scene).** Frost lashes from the altar at everyone within Immediate range. Might defense (Difficulty 4) or take 4 points and all tasks hindered by one step for one round. He visibly ages each time he uses it.
- **Talked Down.** Intellect-based Interaction task (Difficulty 5, eased as above) shakes his certainty and can make the confrontation far less violent.
```

**Combat.** He is not a fighter, and if forced into violence, does it clumsily, almost reluctantly. He doesn't believe he needs protecting. Using Borrowed Cold visibly costs him: he ages before the party's eyes each time, and a perceptive PC notices his hands trembling afterward.

**Interaction.** Cutlip will talk, if given the chance. He wants to be understood, and maybe even wants to be talked out of it, though he'd never admit that. He defends the ritual passionately at first, but a PC who takes his reasons seriously, rather than arguing theology or calling him mad, may find a crack in his certainty (Intellect-based Interaction task, Difficulty 5, eased as above if played sincerely). Success doesn't instantly end the threat, but it visibly shakes him and can make the confrontation at the altar far less violent.

**Arguing with him.** Cutlip has an answer for the easy arguments. The ones that crack him are about what a town actually is.

| The PCs say | Cutlip answers |
|---|---|
| "People will freeze. Crops won't grow." | "People freeze every winter. They come back from it. They don't come back from Ohio." |
| "You can't keep people against their will." | "Nobody chooses to leave. The company chooses for them, every first of March." |
| "This won't bring Caleb back." | "No. But it'll keep the next boy home." |

Arguments like these find the crack (and ease the Interaction task):

- *"The ones who leave carry Helvetia with them. You're keeping the buildings, not the people."*
- *"Look at them. That's not a town. That's a graveyard that's still breathing."*

### Odell Kesling, the Caller

*Level 2 (Level 4 for tasks involving local history and tradition)*

Odell has called the Fasnacht square dance for forty years: same steps, same jokes, same threat to make you do-si-do again if you get it wrong. He is one of the frozen, mid-call, mouth open around a word that never finished. Unlike most of the town's lost souls, his spirit is clear-headed: **Odell walks the Hollow Season** aware and mobile, but unable to return to his body without the party's help.

```creature cypher
Odell Kesling
Level 2 (Level 4 for local history and tradition)
Health: 12 | Armor: 0

> **Lost Soul.** A clear-headed spirit; can't return to his body without the party's help.

- **Damage.** None. Odell will not fight.
```

**Interaction.** Gruff, practical, scared underneath it and covering with orneriness. He knows Fasnacht tradition better than anyone alive and can point the party toward any of the three paths. He trusts the church least: *"Used to be there was a mask for the preacher too. Ain't seen it hung out in six years."*

**Use.** Odell can serve as the party's guide through the Hollow Season (Section 4), offering lore, direction, and a human stake. If the party fails, Odell's spirit fades along with everyone else's.

## 3. Act One: The Fasnacht Ball

### The Parade to the Hall

The party arrives in Helvetia and joins the Fasnacht parade.

**Why They're Here:** Let each player decide, or offer these reasons, which can be mixed across the party:

- **Work in the mines:** They came for work at the mines, or to carry word or goods to the miners, and landed in town the weekend of Fasnacht. Everyone is let off early for it.
- **A break in nature:** They came to the hills to rest, hike, hunt, or get away from something, and the festival caught them on the way through.
- **Passing through:** The road, a broken-down vehicle, or a favor for a friend brought them here, and a stranger pressed a mask into their hands and said "You can't miss Fasnacht."
- **Kin or old ties:** A relative, friend, or old debt in town pulled them back for the season.

Whatever the reason, the town treats them as guests. Nobody is a stranger at Fasnacht.

**The Masks They Made:** In the days before the festival, every visitor is invited to the back room of the old schoolhouse to make a mask of their own, at a table of flour paste, torn newspaper, chicken wire, paint, feathers, and bits of salvage. Have each player describe their mask: what it looks like, what it's made of, and why they made it that way. Offer prompts if they stall:

- Which animal, figure, or feeling did they pick, and is it a face they'd show or one they'd hide behind?
- Is it clean and careful, or rough and lopsided, and what does that say about the maker?
- Is there one detail only they would understand (a color, a scrap of cloth, a name written inside)?

Masks are the one custom at Fasnacht nobody breaks. Have each player put the mask on as the parade begins.

**Read aloud:**

> The afternoon light is already going thin and gold when the horns start up. Somewhere down at the bottom of the holler, a drum takes up a slow beat, and the whole town begins to gather. Doors open one after another. Folks spill out in coats and scarves and, above them, faces: papier-mâché birds, grinning devils, weeping moons, bearded old men, long-nosed foxes. Children run at the edge of the crowd in cruder masks, shrieking, their parents' hands still tacky with paste.
>
> You pull your own mask on, and the world narrows to the shape of its eyeholes. The paste smells like flour and cold. The edges are rough at your cheeks. For a moment it feels like a stranger's face, and then it doesn't.
>
> The parade moves up the road in no particular order: a banjo player walking backward, two men dragging a wagon with a jug of cider and a coffeepot, a woman banging a pot lid with a wooden spoon, somebody's grandmother pushed along in a chair on a hand truck and promenaded as the queen of the whole thing. People call out to one another by guess and by voice, since no one can tell who is who. Smoke curls from chimneys. Breath hangs in the air in small white clouds, and every time somebody laughs, you see it leave them.
>
> At the head of the line, a few of the young folks carry a long pole with a tattered bundle of straw, rags, burlap, and papier-mâché hung from the top: a little scarecrow of winter, a promise of what's coming later. Behind them, past the church and the mine road and the last house before the trees, the sky is going the color of a bruise. You pass the town square, where a great pyre of split kindling stands ready for tonight's bonfire, and then the town hall sign, lanterns already glowing in the windows. The sound of a fiddle tuning up spills out through the open door. Somebody takes your arm and tugs you inside.

Give the players room to talk in character as they walk: who they're marching beside, what they notice, what they think of the masks. Plant small details for later: the bonfire pyre in the square, the mine road at the edge of town, the church steeple with its single window, the tall tree at the tree line, and the Mask-Maker standing bare-faced at the edge of the route, watching the parade go by.

### The Hall

**Read aloud:**

> The Helvetia Town Hall smells like woodsmoke, spilled beer, and the particular sweetness of ramps cooked a dozen different ways. Kerosene lanterns hang from hooks along the rafters. Up near the ceiling, lashed in place with baling wire and old rope, hangs **Old Man Winter**: a great sagging effigy of papier-mâché and burlap, ten feet of him, with a long gray beard of unspun wool and a crown of bent nails standing in for icicles.
>
> Everyone in town is here, and most of them are wearing a mask. Papier-mâché faces, some inherited, some new, all handmade over the winter, turn the whole room into something between a family reunion and a fever dream. Fiddle and banjo cut through the noise. Somebody's calling a square dance. Somebody else is three drinks past dancing and is just spinning in place, laughing.
>
> It is, by every account anyone can give you, a perfectly normal Fasnacht.

Let the players introduce themselves into the scene however fits their characters: dancing, drinking, people-watching, working a corner of the room. Encourage them to create their own NPC in the town that they interact with to better cement themselves into the setting. This is a chance for light roleplay before the horror starts.

Plant the town's worry early. Miners from No. 6 drink to the last good weeks of the season, and somebody always brings up the first of March, when the company posts the spring layoffs. Since the crash, folks are saying No. 6 might not reopen at all. A young man at the cider table tells anyone who'll listen that he's leaving for Akron at the thaw, and his mother, two tables over, pretends she can't hear him.

A PC who asks after the preacher hears that Reverend Cutlip hasn't come to Fasnacht in six years, *"not since his boy."* Nobody says more than that.

Give the players a little time here. Then move to the song.

### The Song: "Please, Old Man"

At some point in the evening, ideally after the players have had a scene or two to breathe, the band slows down and pulls into the song that closes every Fasnacht. It isn't a triumphant song. It's low and bluesy, more dirge than dance tune, sung tired, like a family saying the same grace it's said over a hundred hard winters. It's less a banishment than a prayer: a tally of what winter took, laid out plain, and a plea for it to finally, please, be done.

Odell or another NPC sings it (a low, weary solo voice suits it better than a full-room shout), with the room answering on the refrain. Read it aloud, sing it to an improvised blues tune, or hand it to a player who wants to perform it. The italic lines are stage directions for the GM.

> **"Please, Old Man"**
> *(traditional, Helvetia)*
>
> Well, you stripped the leaves from every tree,
> Left 'em bare as bone for the world to see.
>
> The hillside's gone, nothin' left but me,
> And a chill up my back that won't let me be.
>
> *(The first chill: the breath of everyone in the room turns to fog.)*
>
> Please, old man, ain't you had enough,
> There's nothin' left in the holler to take from us.
>
> Sent your spirits down to the hollow deep,
> Put a fever in the little ones too young to keep,
>
> *(The second chill: the PCs feel their joints stiffen, and the voices around them slow and drag.)*
>
> Froze the well, froze our hearts, and the milk in the pail,
> Froze the road so doc's truck couldn't get up the trail.
>
> We ain't got the strength or the fire to spare,
> We're just askin' winter that you'll leave us here.


### The Freeze

The song ends on its last line, and the band holds the final chord, waiting for a verse no one sings. In that silence, the temperature in the hall drops fast enough that breath fogs mid-word.

**Read aloud:**

> The fiddle player's bow stops moving, still pressed to the string. Odell's mouth is open around the next word, but no sound comes out. Frost blooms across the windows in fast, branching patterns, climbing the glass like something is drawing it from the inside. One by one, then all at once, everyone in the hall goes still: not falling, not collapsing, just *stopping*, mid-motion, mid-laugh, mid-step, their breath fogging once and then not again. A woman is frozen with her hand still raised to her dance partner's. Sara Collins is frozen mid-spin, one foot off the ground.
>
> Above it all, Old Man Winter still hangs from the rafters. And for just a moment, in the dark hollows of his papier-mâché face, something like eyes catch the light.

The player characters are unaffected. Give them a moment to check on the frozen townsfolk: they're alive, breathing shallowly, hearts beating slow. Stopped, not dead. A PC who looks closely at Sara notices she is pregnant.

**GM Intrusion (optional, The Cold Notices):** One PC's own breath fogs once, the cold biting harder than it should, before releasing them. It is a warning that whatever this is could take them too, given time.

### The Mask-Maker Steps Forward

While the party is checking on the frozen, the only other moving thing in the room makes itself known.

**Read aloud:**

> Across the frozen hall, by the door, one figure sticks out like a sore thumb, because he is the only one in the room with nothing covering his face.
>
> He doesn't hurry. He stands a long moment, looking at the stopped fiddle bow, the dancer caught mid-spin, the frost still creeping up the glass. His jaw works like he's chewing something sour. Then his gaze finds you, standing there breathing, and stays.
>
> He pulls a battered notebook from inside his coat, thumbs through it, finds nothing, and shuts it harder than he needs to. "Not once. Not in all the journals. And you lot are awake. Of course you are."
>
> He doesn't ask how. He doesn't seem to want to know. He drags a chair under the rafters, climbs onto a table, and starts working the baling wire loose from Old Man Winter's effigy, careful as a man easing a sleeper into bed. Above him, the papier-mâché face turns slowly on its wire, and for a heartbeat the hollows of its eyes seem to follow him down.
>
> "Don't touch anything," he mutters, without turning his face in your direction.

He lowers the whole effigy, ten feet of burlap and papier-mâché, and stands it upright in the middle of the dance floor, propped on a coat rack and lashed with baling wire. Then he takes the nearest frozen dancer by the shoulders and, gently, turns her to face it. If asked what's going on, he says only *"Fixing it,"* and gives no more explanation here.

**GM Intrusion (The Room Remembers Winter):** As the players process all this, the walls of the hall frost over entirely, the lanterns gutter to nothing, and the door and windows no longer show the town outside, only white, and cold, and distance. The hall has become part of **the Hollow Season**. Move to Section 4.

## 4. Act Two: The Hollow Season

### Crossing Over

When the party steps out of the hall, read aloud:

> It's Helvetia, near enough. But the buildings are sparser than they should be, the colors are wrong, washed down to grays and blues, and the snow isn't falling so much as *hanging*, fat flakes suspended mid-air like the whole world is holding its breath. Your own footprints are the only ones. No wind. No sound, except, very faintly, from somewhere behind you, the last thin thread of a song that never finished.
>
> Looking around, you can still make out the shape of the church steeple, the dark mouth of the mine road up the hollow, and the tree line where the forest starts, marked by the gate naming the trail. Near each of those, faint figures are moving: pale, colorless shapes in glowing masks, repeating something small and human. They don't look up as you pass, but your eyes keep drifting back to them, like a lit window on a dark road.

This is **the Hollow Season**: not quite Helvetia, not quite anywhere else. It is the town as winter remembers it, built from every hard winter the mountain has held onto. It behaves like a dream that takes itself very seriously: consistent, physical, and dangerous, but full of small wrongnesses if the players go looking (a shop sign for a business that closed thirty years ago, a dog that leaves no prints, snow that tastes like nothing at all).

Two kinds of spirit move through it:

- **Lost souls**, the spirits of the townsfolk frozen in the hall tonight, wandering and confused (below).
- **Masked sprites**, moments from winters past, frozen in time and playing out on a loop (see **The Masked Sprites**).

The town square sits at the crossroads at the center of it all, the Fasnacht pyre still standing there unlit. It matters at the end (see **The Burning**, Section 8).

### The Three Landmarks

From just outside the hall, three landmarks are visible in the distance, each faintly lit against the gray:

- **The church steeple**, up the main road, its single window glowing a cold blue-white, brighter than any lamp should manage. *(Path A, Section 5)*
- **The mine entrance**, up the hollow road, where the dark of the tunnel mouth is broken by a deep, banked red glow, like coals under ash. *(Path B, Section 6)*
- **The tree line**, past the last house in town, where one tree stands taller than the rest, its bare branches faintly gold, as if it's holding the last of autumn and hasn't let go. *(Path C, Section 7)*

Any of the three can be reached on foot in a matter of minutes. There's no wrong direction, only a direction not yet chosen.

### The Lost Souls

When the freeze came down, it pulled the spirits of the frozen townsfolk loose from their bodies, and they wander the Hollow Season like sleepwalkers: translucent, like breath on cold glass, still wearing their Fasnacht masks. Most are confused and half-aware, unable to hold a conversation for more than a line or two. Use them as color between landmarks:

- **Sara Collins's spirit**, still in her dancing shoes, wandering the main road calling her husband's name. She asks a PC whether they've seen him, and doesn't understand the answer.
- **The fiddler**, sitting on a frozen fence rail, bowing a fiddle that isn't there, humming the song over and over and always stopping at the same place.
- **A pair of children**, holding hands, following the party at a distance and ducking out of sight when looked at.

Every time the Mask-Maker turns a townsperson in the hall, one of the lost souls somewhere flickers and grows fainter. A PC who watches long enough sees it happen.

**Odell's spirit** is the exception: clear-headed, ornery, and very much himself. He finds the party soon after they cross over, looking exactly as he did mid-call. He knows the town and its history better than anyone, and he can offer this:

> "Church folks got quiet the last few years. Reverend Cutlip stopped comin' to Fasnacht altogether, ever since his boy drowned at the ford walkin' out to Ohio. Blames spring for it, I reckon, and he ain't the only one in this town who does. Old No. 4's been closed since the roof fell on the lower seam, but old-timers always said somethin' down there kept the lights burnin' even after. And that tree, hell, that tree was here before the town was. My granddad used to leave it a plate at first frost, every year, like it was kin."

If asked about the song, Odell can tell them it has a last verse, the preacher's verse, and that the preacher always led it. *"I've heard it forty times. Can't hold onto the words in here. It's like they freeze before they get to my mouth."*

Neither Odell nor anyone else will choose for the party, and no one calls one path "safer" than another. They aren't, meaningfully. The choice should be about tone and what the table finds interesting.

### The Masked Sprites

Scattered around the Hollow Season, especially near the three landmarks, are **masked sprites**: faint, colorless figures from winters long past, each one a moment the mountain froze and kept, playing out over and over. Each one's mask glows softly. They are the points of interest of the Hollow Season, and the party is drawn to them the way people are drawn to a lit window on a dark road.

**How the sprites work:**

- Each scene is a short loop of about a minute. The sprites don't notice the party unless addressed.
- A PC who speaks to a sprite gets one line in character, then the sprite returns to its loop. Sprites can't be fought, hurt, or led away. (The congregation inside the church, Section 5, is the exception.)
- Each sprite's mask is a stray in the Mask-Maker's sense. When a loop ends, the mask is shed, and he picks it up, complains about it, and carries it back to set on a chair facing the effigy.
- Each scene points to one path and shows something true about it, and each one quietly demonstrates one of the Wake Customs (Section 8). The sprites do not choose for the party.

**At the Church: The Congregation of 1918**

> Figures come down the main road in a loose, silent procession, every one of them in a plain white mask, all identical. Their coats are old-fashioned, their boots hobnailed. Several of them carry something long between them, wrapped in a sheet. At the head of the line, holding the church door open with one hand, stands a gaunt, gray-haired man with no mask at all. He greets each of them by name, quietly, as they pass: *"Come in, Ruth. Come in, Dale. It's warm in here."* One by one, they climb the steps and go inside, and the blue-white light in the steeple window brightens slightly with each one.

This is a memory of the winter of 1918, when influenza came through the hollows faster than the frozen ground could be broken to bury the dead. The man at the door is **Reverend Josiah Cutlip**, Asa Cutlip's father. Among the marchers, carrying one end of a sheeted body, is a younger Asa himself. A PC who speaks to him gets: *"We're only keeping them a little longer. Come spring, we'll have to let them go. Just a little longer."* A PC who later meets Asa (Section 5) recognizes both faces.

This scene should feel unsettling rather than mysterious: whatever happens at the church happens on purpose, and with willing feet. *(Wake clue: he names each guest as they come in, which is **the Telling**.)*

**At the Mine: Break Time**

> A string of small red points glows in the dark by the mouth of the mine road. As you get closer, they resolve into the burning tips of cigarettes. Four men in old gear, black paper masks pushed up over their foreheads like welders' goggles, are sitting on a stack of rotted timbers, sharing a thermos. Their carbide lamps are turned low. The smoke doesn't drift the way smoke should. It hangs in the air like the snow does.
>
> *"Foreman finds us out here, he'll have our hides."*
> *"Foreman's down in the lower seam, Earl. He ain't coming up."*
> *"Told you that roof was talking. Didn't I tell you?"*
> *"You tell everybody everything. Pass the thermos."*

This is the last winter shift before the roof came down on the lower seam, thirty-odd years ago. The loop ends with one of them flicking his cigarette into the dark and the others standing up, pulling their masks down, and walking into the tunnel. A PC who speaks to one gets a gruff, friendly line (*"Ain't supposed to be up here. You didn't see us."*) and, if they're respectful, may be offered a cigarette. A PC who accepts has their first Interaction with **Tobias Wolfe** (Section 6) eased by one step.

This scene is older and more ordinary than the church's procession: the same crew, the same break, every day, whether anyone is watching or not. *(Wake clue: the lamps kept burning low in the dark, which is **the Light**.)*

**At the Tree Line: The Offering**

> Near the base of the gold-branched tree, a boy of about nine sits in the snow with a small plate in his lap, scowling at it. A crude fox mask is pushed up on his forehead. A woman stands over him, bundled in a long coat and a pale, round-cheeked mask painted with a patient smile.
>
> *"Why do I gotta? It's a tree."*
> *"Because your granddad did it, and his daddy did it before him."*
> *"That's not a reason."*
> *"It's the best kind there is, baby. Tradition ain't about the tree. It's about the folks who stood right where you're standing, and what they went without so we could be here. You honor that, and they're still with you."*
> *"...It's just a plate of cornbread."*
> *"Then it's a good one. Go on."*
>
> The boy sighs enormously, gets up, sets the plate against the roots, and bows his head. The mother puts her hand on his shoulder. The scene begins again, with a small difference: a bit more time passes between the lines, and this time, only the boy comes, older now. The next time, the plate is already there, and no one comes at all. Then the snow in front of the tree sits empty for a long time before the loop resets.

The boy is Odell's granddad, in a winter long before the town had a mine. If Odell's spirit is with the party, he goes quiet, then says only, *"Well. I'll be. That's Pap."* A PC who speaks to the boy gets: *"Are you gonna make me do it too?"* A PC who speaks to his mother gets: *"He'll understand when he's older. They always do."*

This one is meant to sting: it shows a generations-long tradition that has visibly stopped, a debt that stopped being paid without anyone deciding to stop. If a PC remembers the mother's words when they meet **Granny Greenbriar** (Section 7), Interaction tasks there are eased by one step. *(Wake clue: the plate set out for a guest, which is **the Table**.)*

None of the three scenes tells the party exactly what they'll find, but each should color the choice: the church promises a willing conspiracy and a personal grief, the mine promises something old and patient laboring in the dark, and the forest promises a relationship neglected rather than broken.

### The Mask-Maker's Vigil

Once the party is out in the Hollow Season, the Mask-Maker is *always* somewhere in view if they look: a distant, bare-faced figure moving unhurried between the landmarks, never quite where the party expects. He is working, and the work looks a great deal like tending a graveyard. Spread these across however long the party spends here:

- Kneeling in the suspended snow to lift a white mask shed by the church procession, frost-rimed, and slipping it into his satchel.
- Standing at the mouth of the mine road for a long moment with his head bowed, as though listening to something no one else can hear, before shaking his head and walking away.
- Pressing a bare palm toward the tree at the tree line from too far away to have actually touched it, and holding it there.

If a PC approaches and asks what he's doing, he says only: *"Every year somebody's mask ends up somewhere it shouldn't. I'm just seeing they get found."* Pressed harder, he gives the warning for whichever landmark is nearest:

> **The church:** "The church will give you an answer, but it'll cost you someone's faith to get it."
> **The mine:** "The mine will give you fire, but fire wants feeding, and it won't much care what you feed it."
> **The tree:** "The tree will give you spring, but spring always was a debt as much as a gift, if anyone bothered to remember."

**If the party confronts him** about what he's really doing, he answers truthfully and without hurry:

> "Winter's got no face this year, and a thing with no face can't be told to go. A face is only a face when somebody's looking at it. So I'm seeing that everybody looks. Wasn't how I meant to spend the night, and I don't need company for it. Keepers have done this every year since before your grandparents were born. I've done it six. It's never gone like this."

He will explain the Turning of the Faces in general terms if asked (Section 2), but he won't volunteer what it costs the frozen townsfolk. A PC who asks directly (*"Does this hurt them?"*) gets a true answer: *"Every one I turn gives him a little of their breath. It's slower than the cold would've been, and it's the only way he gets a body."* This is the moment the party learns that the clock is real, that he is part of it, and that he is not the enemy but is not on their side either.

### Frost Wisps (Optional Encounter)

If you want a mechanical beat before the party commits, or if they linger too long deciding, **Frost Wisps** drift out of the suspended snow: small fragments of Old Man Winter's attention, come to see who's moving through his season uninvited.

```creature cypher
Frost Wisp
Level 2
Health: 6 | Armor: 0 | Movement: Short

> **Dispersal.** Any solid hit disperses one. It re-forms elsewhere later.

- **Damage.** 2 points. Might defense or all physical tasks are hindered by one step.

> **Chilled.** Physical tasks hindered by one step until you warm yourself at an open flame or leave the Hollow Season.
```

**Combat.** Wisps have no real malice. They drift rather than walk, and are closer to curious cold fronts than hunters. Use two or three at once for a quick taste of danger, not a serious fight.

**Interaction.** None. They understand only cold and warmth.

This encounter is a reminder that the Hollow Season is dangerous and time-limited. Don't let it run long.

### Choosing a Path

The party may go to any of the three landmarks, in any order, and return to the open ground between them at any time. Nothing closes behind them. Odell's spirit, if present, stays with them and offers what he knows.

- **Path A: The Watchers in the Nave** (Section 5), the church.
- **Path B: What Sleeps in the Seam** (Section 6), the mine.
- **Path C: The Sap Still Running** (Section 7), the forest.

## 5. Path A: The Watchers in the Nave

*A congregation of winters past, and a living man who sang winter an invitation. Key: the True Final Verse.*

### Arrival

**Read aloud:**

> The church door is standing open, and warm light, actual warm light, gold instead of that cold blue-white, spills out onto the steps, along with the sound of singing. Not the ragged, unfinished song from the Ball. Something slower, older, sung by voices that all know the words by heart. Up close, the blue-white glow you saw from a distance turns out to be coming from inside, from the tall window behind the altar, like there's a second, colder moon shining directly through the glass.
>
> Every pew is full. Every single person in them is wearing the same plain white mask you saw on the road, and the same old-fashioned coats. Along the side aisle, under sheets, lie long, still shapes, laid out on boards. The congregation sings without looking around, without seeming to notice you at all.

The warmth is real, and it should feel wrong precisely because it's the first comfortable temperature the party has felt since the freeze: an inviting, sleepy warmth that makes you want to sit down in a back pew and stop worrying for a minute. **Any PC who lingers in the nave for more than a few minutes without acting must make an Intellect defense task (Difficulty 3)** or find themselves sitting, entranced, humming along, until shaken by an ally.

### The Congregation

These are the people of Helvetia in 1918, the congregation that first sang the Litany of the Long Winter over their own unburied dead. They are a memory, like the other sprites, but the hymn has given them weight: here, and only here, they can touch and be touched. They are neighbors, not monsters. Some are people frozen in the hall tonight, twelve years younger. Others are the dead they were mourning. The adventure plays better if the party realizes that quickly.

```creature cypher
The Congregation
Level 3 (as a group)
Health: 18 | Armor: 0 | Movement: Short

> **One Mob.** Shared Health pool. Or split into two or three clusters of Health 6 each for a more granular fight.
> **Bound to Cutlip.** They stop entirely, going glassy and sitting back down, the moment Reverend Cutlip is incapacitated, convinced, or removed from the room.

- **Damage.** 2 points (a firm, unthinking shove or grip meant to keep intruders from the altar, not to injure).
- **Unmasked.** A PC who stops one and removes its mask frees that soul; it fades. Slower than fighting, but no one is hurt.
```

**Combat.** The congregation acts only to block the aisle or restrain someone reaching for the altar or the Reverend. They use no weapons and do not pursue outside the church.

**Interaction.** Unresponsive while the hymn plays. A PC who stops one and removes their mask frees that soul: the figure sighs, looks toward the shapes under the sheets, and fades, finally let go. This can be done instead of fighting through the whole congregation, at the cost of time.

**GM Intrusion (A Familiar Face):** As a PC moves to bypass or subdue a member of the congregation, they recognize the face under the mask: the shopkeeper who sold them cider today, or someone kind to them at the Ball, twelve years younger and grieving. The action doesn't become impossible, but let it cost something (hesitation, a beat of guilt).

### Reverend Asa Cutlip

At the altar, leading the hymn, stands **Reverend Asa Cutlip** (full details and stats in Section 2): gaunt, soft-spoken, wearing no mask, and utterly convinced he is doing the most loving thing he has ever done. He is the only living man in the room. A PC who saw the 1918 procession recognizes his father's face in his, and the younger man who carried the dead.

He will talk before he fights. If the party gives him the chance, read or paraphrase:

> "Do you know what happens here on the first of March? The company tacks a list to the store door. Half the names on it have been working that seam since they were fourteen. By the time the sarvis blooms, they're gone: Wheeling, Akron, Pittsburgh, anywhere with a mill. Their wives follow, and then their children. I've stood at the end of that road and blessed every one of them, most of my life. I blessed my own boy. He didn't make it past the ford.
>
> "Winter is the only season this town is whole. The mine runs, the men come home at night, there's coal in every stove and a full table in every house. So I asked it to stay. I know what I asked for. I'm not a fool. A town that can't change. A town that can't grow. But it's *here*. Every soul in it is still here. Tell me what spring was going to give them that's better than that."

Use **Arguing with him** (Section 2) for how he answers back. If they come at him instead, the congregation closes ranks and he uses **Borrowed Cold**.

### The Altar and the Litany

Behind Cutlip, the altar holds an old leather hymnal, open to a page that doesn't match the rest of the book: older paper, hand-copied, titled at the top in a shaky, careful hand, **The Litany of the Long Winter.**

Through searching, through Interaction with Cutlip, or with an Intellect task to study the hymnal (Difficulty 4), the party can piece together the truth:

> "Please, Old Man" has a final verse that sends winter off for the year, and by custom the preacher leads it. For six years, nobody has sung it. Tonight, at the hour the town sang the song without its ending, Cutlip sang his father's Litany instead, an invitation rather than a farewell, and Old Man Winter accepted.

The true final verse is written on a separate, loose page tucked into the hymnal's back cover, plain and easy to miss (Intellect task, Difficulty 3, to notice it while examining the book). **This page is the path's key:** whoever carries it can sing the verse during the final fight (Section 8). Hand the players the True Final Verse from the Appendix when they find it.

### Resolving the Path

The scene can end several ways, and none of them requires a fight to the death:

- **Confrontation:** If the party fights through the congregation and stops Cutlip (0 Health, or otherwise subdued), the hymn collapses, the congregation goes still, and the true verse can be taken freely from the altar.
- **Persuasion:** If a PC reaches Cutlip emotionally, he hands over the true verse himself, quietly. He asks only one thing: that when the roads open and the first family leaves, somebody stands at the ford and sees them across, the way nobody did for Caleb. This is the saddest version of the scene, and for the right table, the most powerful.
- **Theft:** A stealthy party might slip past the congregation and Cutlip entirely, take the page while the hymn continues, and leave before anyone's the wiser. It denies the table a confrontation with Cutlip, but it is a valid, quieter way through.

Once the true verse is in the party's hands, the church dims, its warmth fading back to the Hollow Season's gray cold. Return to the Hollow Season (Section 4) to choose another path, or move to the climax (Section 8).

## 6. Path B: What Sleeps in the Seam

*A dead crew still working, and something old and warm banked in the dark. Key: the Everburning Ember.*

### Arrival

**Read aloud:**

> The mine road climbs steeper than it has any right to, gravel giving way to old rail ties half-swallowed by frozen mud. The tunnel mouth is timbered with black, split beams, and the red glow you saw from a distance comes from somewhere deep inside: not firelight exactly, more like the memory of firelight, banked and patient, the way coals look at four in the morning after everyone's gone to bed. It's warmer here than anywhere else in the Hollow Season. Not comfortable-warm. Forge-warm. The kind of warm that comes from something working very hard, very slowly, for a very long time.
>
> A rusted sign, barely legible, reads: HELVETIA NO. 4, CLOSED. Someone has scratched under it, more recently than the rust: *STILL WORKING.*

The mine is a physical, hazardous place, and this path should feel more like a descent than a debate. Lean into claustrophobia, heat, and the sense of something vast and slow moving just out of sight.

### The Parting

A few hundred feet in, the main heading splits in two. Both branches come out at the lower seam, so the party only needs to take one.

**Read aloud:**

> The heading splits around a timber post as thick as a man. Nailed to it is a fireboss's board, a slab of slate where the morning's gas checks were chalked before every shift. The chalk is thirty years old and should be long gone, but it's as crisp as if it were written this morning.
>
> To the left, a low airway runs off into the dark, so low you'd have to stoop. The air coming out of it doesn't move at all. To the right, the haulage road follows the rails on toward the red glow, and a faint, warm draft breathes out of it, almost friendly.

The board reads **LEFT: BAD AIR. NO LAMPS.** On the right, the old warning, **GAS. NO FLAME.**, has been smeared out with a thumb, and **ALL CLEAR** is chalked over it in a fresh, careless hand. A PC who studies the board (Intellect task, Difficulty 3) notices the new chalk is still warm.

The two branches are two different kinds of danger. One puts out a flame. The other is waiting for one.

### The Return Airway: The Black Damp

The slow, careful branch. It is built on skill tasks and dread rather than a fight.

**The Black Damp:** A low stretch of tunnel where the air has gone bad: old, spent air that won't hold a flame or a breath for long. A Might task (Difficulty 3) gets a character through before their vision starts to gray at the edges. Characters carrying open flame see their light gutter here, a good, quiet, dread-building beat.

**The Collapsed Crosscut:** Past the bad air, the airway turns through a passage half-choked with old timber and rockfall. A Speed task (Difficulty 4) picks through without bringing more of the ceiling down. Failure deals 2 points of damage from falling debris and makes noise that draws attention deeper in.

**GM Intrusion (The Mountain Notices):** Distant, rhythmic knocking echoes through the rock, three knocks, a pause, three knocks again, and dust sifts down from the ceiling in time with it. Nothing attacks. It doesn't need to.

Beyond the crosscut, the airway opens into the lower seam. Move to **Tobias Wolfe and the Shift**.

### The Main Haulage: The Firedamp Blast

The faster, warmer branch. It is a trap, and it ends in a fight.

**The Canary Station**

**Read aloud:**

> The haulage road runs straight and easy along the rails, and the warm draft grows with every step. Ahead, a cluster of low red lights is gathered beside the track, and for a moment it looks like the break-time crew from up top: a few figures sitting on an upturned coal car, hunched over, resting.
>
> They aren't miners. They're shaped from embers, like a fire that's learned to sit, and they glow and dim with something like breath. Beside them, on a shelf cut into the rib, hang a row of small wire cages. In every one, a canary lies on the floor of the cage, feet up, quite still. One of the ember-figures reaches through the bars and strokes a dead bird with one glowing finger, gently, the way you'd pet a cat.

These are **the Kindled** (stats below). A PC who knows anything about mining, or who makes an Intellect task (Difficulty 2), knows what dead canaries mean: there is gas in this tunnel, and a lot of it.

**The Borrowed Faces**

When the party gets close, the Kindled stand and turn, each one wearing the face of someone from town. They don't attack. They beckon, backing away around the bend and calling the party deeper. Use any townsfolk the party met tonight, or improvise one-liners (*"the old man who sold you a mask string," "the girl who laughed at your dancing," "a miner who toasted the last good weeks of the season"*). The faces don't hold for long. Each one melts back into embers and reforms as someone else, but for that moment, it is unmistakably *them*, calling the party deeper.

**The Bell**

Around the bend, the roof rises into a **bell**: a dome-shaped hollow where rock fell out of the ceiling years ago, leaving a cavity where natural gas, lighter than air, has collected like water in an upturned bowl. The Kindled try to draw the party underneath it. The moment a PC steps beneath the dome, they reach up and set it off.

A PC who is watching the ceiling, or who already understood the canaries, can spot the bell before stepping under it (Intellect task, Difficulty 3, eased by one step if they read the canaries correctly). A party that spots it can try to cross under it one at a time at a dead run, rush the Kindled before they can reach the ceiling, or smother them first. Whatever they do, the Kindled try to set it off as soon as anyone is underneath.

**Read aloud (when it goes):**

> The Kindled stop smiling, all at once. One of them stretches up, longer than any arm should go, and touches the roof. The air changes before the sound does, a pressure, a held breath, and then the dome above you goes white-orange, and the blast hits like a hand shoving flat against your whole body. Lanterns gutter out. Dust and grit rain from the ceiling. For a moment, everything is roaring and dark at once.
>
> When it settles, the dark isn't empty. The ember-figures are coming back together out of the burning ceiling, brighter than before, and they still have their borrowed faces on.

Any PC under the bell takes 5 points of damage (Speed defense task, Difficulty 4, halves this). Everyone else within Short range takes 3 points (Speed defense task, Difficulty 3, halves this). Then 2-4 **Kindled**, fed by the blast, re-form and attack, scaling with party size. Past the bell, the haulage road runs down into the lower seam.

```creature cypher
The Kindled
Level 3
Health: 4 | Armor: 0 | Movement: Short

> **Living Flame.** Immune to ordinary physical damage. Snow, ice, water, or a smothering action (Might task, Difficulty 2) extinguishes one outright.

- **Damage.** 3 points of fire on a successful grab. Intellect defense (Difficulty 3) or Transfixed. Grabbed PCs lose 1 Might each round until they break free.

> **Transfixed.** Held by the borrowed face, unable to act for a round.
```

**Combat.** The Kindled don't attack to kill. They drift rather than walk, and cling, seeking warmth. Blows pass through them like a hand through a flame. They are not cruel. They are barely anything at all: fragments of shock and old fire from the day the roof came down, wearing whatever face will draw someone closer.

**Interaction.** They can't really speak, but the borrowed face sometimes shapes a word or two in a voice that isn't quite right (*"come home," "it's warmer this way," "don't leave me"*). It is not communication, just an echo doing what echoes do.

**GM Intrusion (A Face That Matters):** For a moment, one of the Kindled wears the face of someone the party has met and cared about tonight: Sara Collins, Odell, a dancer they spoke to at the Ball, or a PC's own connection to the town. It changes nothing mechanically. It should change quite a lot at the table.

If the party came through the haulage, Tobias Wolfe (below) says plainly what these things were when they reach him, with quiet relief that they made it through:

> "Now those ain't us. Those are just what's left of the moment we died, never settled the way my crew did. Don't let 'em fool you, and don't let 'em latch on too long. There's kindness in leavin' 'em be, if you can manage it. Snow'll see to 'em without you havin' to hurt anything that used to be somebody."

### Tobias Wolfe and the Shift

Where the two branches meet in the lower seam, the miners from the break-time loop are no longer a silent memory. The shift is here, along with the foreman they were dodging, and he is willing to talk.

**Tobias Wolfe**, foreman of the crew lost when the roof came down on the lower seam thirty-odd years back, is a spirit in old-fashioned mining gear, carbide lamp still lit on his cap, entirely calm about being dead. He and his crew have kept "working" ever since, not out of any curse, but because the alternative was simply stopping, and miners don't much care for stopping. He does not fight.

> "We ain't haints, if that's what's got you spooked. Just fellas who never clocked out proper. Somethin's down here older'n us, though, older'n the mine, even. Been banked up quiet for years. Guess your winter problem's got it stirred up enough to maybe lend a hand. Might could ask it. Might could also just take what it's got and run. Wouldn't recommend it, but I ain't gonna stop you either."

Tobias guides the party to the bottom of the seam, where the Banked Fire waits, if treated with basic respect: a little conversation, acknowledgment of his crew, no desecration of the old workings. If the party is dismissive, destructive, or disrespectful, he stops helping and fades. The party can still find their way, but without a guide, and without a warning about what comes next.

### The Banked Fire

At the bottom of the lower seam, in a chamber the collapse somehow never touched, something old and slow and warm is waiting. It isn't fire, quite. It is closer to fire's memory, the way Old Man Winter is closer to winter's memory than to actual cold. Generations of labor, warmth, and lives spent in this dark have banked themselves here into something with just enough shape to be spoken to.

```creature cypher
The Banked Fire
Level 6
Health: 24 | Armor: 2

> **Old and Patient.** Health reflects how hard it is to resist, not a combat threat. It will not fight under ordinary circumstances.
> **Hears Lies.** Honest bargaining is eased by one step. Deceiving or manipulating it is hindered by two steps.

- **Damage.** None in ordinary interaction. If provoked or robbed, scorches one target for 5 points without moving.
- **Taken, Not Given.** If the ember is taken by force or trickery, its carrier makes a Might defense (Difficulty 5) or takes 6 points.
```

**Interaction.** Speaks slowly and plainly, without malice and without particular warmth, despite what it is. It has been patient for longer than the mine has existed and sees no reason to hurry. It does not lie, but it does not volunteer what it isn't asked.

The Banked Fire will lend its heat against Old Man Winter, but, as the Mask-Maker warned, **fire wants feeding, and it won't much care what you feed it.** It states its price plainly, without cruelty: something warm and real, given over freely. Let the party choose or improvise an offering. Some options:

- **A memory of real warmth:** A PC recounts, in detail, a genuinely warm memory (comfort, safety, love) and permanently loses the emotional clarity of it, though not the facts. From now on, the memory is flat and distant. This is a roleplay cost, not a stat penalty.
- **A portion of vitality:** A PC gives bodily warmth: reduce their Might Pool by a meaningful amount, recovered through rest after the adventure.
- **An object of real value:** Something a PC has carried a long way and cares about (a family heirloom, a well-used tool) is given over and consumed.

If the party tries to take its power by force or trickery instead, it doesn't fight back directly. It simply gives *too much*: whoever carries the ember must make a Might defense task (Difficulty 5) or take 6 points of damage as it burns hotter than intended. Fire taken rather than given tends to take something back.

### The Everburning Ember

Whatever the price, the Banked Fire yields a single, small coal, warm to the touch and never cooling. It never burns whoever carries it unless mishandled. **This is the path's key.**

**An Everburning Ember**
*Cypher, Level 5*

*Effect:* In the final fight (Section 8), touched to a candle, it lights all four candles at once for **the Light**, and Winter can't snuff them for the rest of the fight. It can be used only once.

### Resolving the Path

- **The Fair Bargain:** Paying the price honestly yields the ember cleanly, along with a rare moment of something like warmth from the spirit itself: a small, tired acknowledgment that the debt is settled.
- **The Theft:** Taking the ember without paying is possible but costly (see above), and leaves the Banked Fire diminished and resentful.
- **Tobias's Favor:** If the party treated Tobias and his crew with respect throughout, he and his crew "walk the party back up," their lantern light making the return trip safe and skipping any remaining obstacles.

Once the ember is in hand, the deep heat of the mine recedes behind the party as they climb, and the Hollow Season's gray cold reasserts itself. Return to the Hollow Season (Section 4) to choose another path, or move to the climax (Section 8).

## 7. Path C: The Sap Still Running

*A neglected tree, a stag on winter's leash, and a debt the town forgot to pay. Key: the Sarvis Berry.*

### Arrival

**Read aloud:**

> The trees close in slowly, not like a wall but like a held breath. The snow out here hasn't been touched by anyone: no footprints but your own, not even animal tracks, though you pass a fox frozen mid-leap over a fallen log, and further on a bird stopped dead in the air a few feet off the ground, wings spread, going nowhere. Everything is holding still, the way the Ball did, except out here it feels less like an interruption and more like the natural order of things, like the forest has always been a little bit sleeping, and tonight it just sleeps a little too well.
>
> Ahead, past the last of the ordinary trees, stands the one you saw from town: taller, older, its bare branches faintly gold. Up close, you can see the ice. It has climbed the trunk in thick, glassy sheets, creeping up toward the last of that gold like a hand slowly closing.

This path is quieter and lonelier than the other two: less about opposition and more about neglect, distance, and remembering something the town let itself forget. Let silence do a lot of the work.

### Into the Hush

Use two or three of these as the party makes their way toward the tree:

- **The Long Hush:** The forest's unnatural quiet muffles more than sound. An Intellect task (Difficulty 3) is needed to notice anything approaching or changing nearby before it's already close. Failure causes no harm by itself, but it should make the players feel their senses can't be trusted out here.
- **Widow's Lace:** A frozen creek crossed by ice thin enough to see the black water moving underneath. A Speed task (Difficulty 4) crosses safely. Failure drops a character through to the waist, cold enough that they lose their next action getting out and must warm up before continuing.

  > The frozen creek is sturdy enough to stand on but thin enough to see through. Black water moves slowly underneath, and caught in it, like flies in glass, are shapes you'd rather not look at: pale masks, drifting just under the surface, faces looking up.
  >
  > You've gone a third of the way across when the ice shivers. Not a crack. A shudder, like something large has turned over in its sleep beneath you.

- **The Watching Trees:** More than once, a PC glances away and back to find a nearby tree standing at a slightly different angle than before. It is never caught in the act and never aggressive, just wrong. This is texture, not a threat; don't make it mechanical.

### The Frost Stag

Between the party and the tree stands its old companion, twisted into a jailer. Once, by every account Odell or the old-timers might give, a huge, pale stag kept easy company with the tree, appearing at first frost alongside the townsfolk who came to leave offerings. This year, with no one coming and the tree's strength fading, Old Man Winter reached it first: its antlers grown through with clear ice, a thin frozen collar visible at its throat if anyone looks closely, its eyes gone the same flat blue-white as the church window. It isn't cruel. It's *worn like a leash.*

```creature cypher
The Frost Stag
Level 5
Health: 22 | Armor: 1 | Movement: Long

> **Frozen Collar.** A called shot at the collar (Difficulty 5) shatters it in one solid hit, ending the fight without harming the stag.
> **Not to Kill.** A PC reduced to 0 Health in this fight is driven back and stunned rather than seriously harmed.

- **Damage.** 5 points (a rack of icy antlers, lowered and charging).
- **Calmed.** A PC who approaches slowly, weapons sheathed, can calm it (Intellect- or Might-based Interaction, Difficulty 5) and break the collar by hand.
```

**Combat.** The Stag fights to keep the party from the tree, not to kill. Its rime of ice serves as armor, and it is fast and uses the trees against pursuers. When the collar shatters, the Stag staggers, shakes itself like a dog coming out of water, and walks away into the trees, free.

**Interaction.** The Stag can't speak or be reasoned with directly. But a PC who approaches slowly, without weapons drawn, and calms it may avoid the fight altogether and get close enough to break the collar by hand.

**GM Intrusion (Old Kindness):** Mid-fight, the Stag hesitates, lowering its head not to charge but almost to nuzzle, some old memory of gentleness fighting through the ice, before winter's grip reasserts. This is a good moment to let a compassionate PC try something creative.

### The Sleeping Tree

With the Stag freed or driven off, the party can reach the tree itself.

**Read aloud:**

> The trees fall away, and you step into a clearing that is not empty. Stone markers rise out of the snow in uneven rows, some tall and carved, some plain fieldstones, some just a flat rock with a name scratched in with a nail. Generations of Helvetia lie here. And at the center of it all, the tree.
>
> It is far bigger than it looked from town. The trunk is wider than the church door, and its roots rise out of the ground like knuckles and run beneath every grave, slipping under stones and around markers as if holding each one by the hand. Its gold branches stretch over the whole graveyard like a roof.
>
> The ice has climbed most of the way up. It seals the bark in glassy sheets, and under it you can see faces in the grain: not carved, not painted, but pressed up from inside, a thousand soft impressions of brows and mouths and closed eyes. A faint gold light moves slowly through the wood, from root to branch and back, like a heartbeat through a frozen window.
>
> Then you notice the stones. The newest ones are crisp and legible. The older ones are blurred. The oldest are blank. Where the frost has crept over the names, the faces in the bark are fading too, one by one, going smooth.

This is **Granny Greenbriar**, not a name so much as what the mountain and the town have called this presence for longer than anyone can trace. She is root-kin to whatever spring actually is in these mountains: a season's memory given shape, the same as Old Man Winter and the Banked Fire. She does not fight.

She does not wake easily, and she does not wake angry. What she needs isn't a bargain; it's an acknowledgment. The boy and his mother at the tree line showed exactly what's missing: for generations, someone came at first frost, left something, and stood a moment with their head bowed. Then no one came. Before anything else, **the tree needs to know she wasn't simply forgotten**, that someone still remembers, and means it.

There is no fixed mechanical solution here; reward genuine roleplay. Any of these can work if played sincerely:

- A PC leaves something of real value at the roots (food, a keepsake, anything that costs them something to give) and speaks honestly about what winter has cost the town this year, echoing the song's litany of harms. This should feel like a small, real moment, not a skill check.
- A PC performs the old first-frost tradition properly. Odell's spirit can supply it if asked: *"Just gotta tell her what you lost. She always did like knowin' she still mattered to somebody."*
- Failing either of those, allow a single Intellect-based Interaction task (Difficulty 6, reflecting how much rides on sincerity over technique) to speak honestly and be heard.

However it's played, the ice over the trunk cracks and slides away in sheets, and the gold light beneath brightens, slow and warm, like a long-held breath finally let out.

### The First Fruit

Granny Greenbriar offers no lecture, no price, no further test. She turns one bare branch toward the party, and where it brushes the air, a single piece of fruit swells into being, small and impossible for the season: a **sarvis berry**, dark and ripe. Sarvis blooms white along the ridgelines at the first true thaw, when, by old tradition, the circuit preacher finally rides back through the hollows to bury the winter's dead. The berry is faintly warm, like something just picked in full sun. **This is the path's key.**

**A Single Sarvis Berry**
*Cypher, Level 4*

*Effect:* Spring compressed into a heartbeat. Use it one of three ways:

- **In the final fight (Section 8):** Laid on the plate at the head of the table, it seats Winter for **the Table** without a roll, and he stays seated even if struck.  Eating the berry melts his armor away and soaks him, requiring a turn to restore his previous rigor.
- **On a frozen ally or object:** It thaws them instantly and heals completely, no roll required.  This includes NPCs who the players may want to join the fight as their souls can rebound to their bodies.

There is only ever one, and it can be used only once.

### Resolving the Path

- **The Freed Guardian:** If the Stag was freed rather than beaten, it follows the party back through the forest at a distance, a final, wordless image of something old set right.
- **The Honest Offering:** Let what the party gave or said carry weight into the ending. The sarvis berry can carry a faint trace of it: a color, a scent, a warmth that reminds them of what they gave up.
- **A Quieter Ending:** Nothing here needs to be taken by force or paid for. This path resolves through remembering rather than winning. Let the table breathe in it a moment before moving on.

Once the berry is in hand, the tree's gold light dims to something gentler, and the forest fades back to the Hollow Season's gray. Return to the Hollow Season (Section 4) to choose another path, or move to the climax (Section 8).

## 8. Act Three: The Climax

### Back to the Hall

The party returns to the frozen hall when they choose to, or when you judge the clock has run out. If they linger too long, the Hollow Season itself draws them back: every road leads to the hall door.

**Read aloud:**

> The hall is as you left it, and not. Everyone's still frozen: the fiddle player, mid-note; Odell, mid-call; the woman with her hand still raised. But they've been moved. Every one of them has been turned where they stand, shuffled around on stiff feet, so that the whole town faces the same way.
>
> In the middle of the dance floor, lashed upright to the rafters with baling wire, stands Old Man Winter's effigy, ten feet of burlap and papier-mâché, its wool beard hanging to its knees. Every masked face in the hall is looking at it: birds, devils, weeping moons, long-nosed foxes, hundreds of painted eyes. Along the walls, on chairs that were empty when you left, sit old, frost-rimed masks with no one behind them, looking at it too.
>
> The Mask-Maker has his hands on the shoulders of the last one: a dancer, still a foot off the ground, frozen mid-spin. He is turning her, slowly, on the toe of one shoe.

### The Turning of the Faces

This is the ritual's last step. The Mask-Maker is seconds from turning the last dancer to face the effigy, and then he will sing. It is the party's last chance to choose how this ends. Give them time to react. He doesn't stop unless he's made to.

What he says first, quietly, without looking up:

> "Oh, you came back. I'd hoped you'd have the sense to stay out of it. Well. This is the last one. Then the song. Whatever happens after, it's not going the way it's supposed to. Be ready."

The party has three ways to answer him:

- **Let him finish.** He turns the last dancer, steps back, and sings the stanza. The ritual completes cleanly, and Old Man Winter manifests at full strength (see **Old Man Winter's Stats**, below). The Mask-Maker says only, *"Well. Whatever you've got, now's the time. Don't make a mess."*
- **Interrupt him.** Talking him down, grabbing his arm, or breaking into the song is an Interaction or Speed task, Difficulty 5. On a success, the song stops short and Winter comes in **Half-Called**: he loses his first action getting his bearings, but he doesn't fit the body and doesn't know whom he's speaking to, so every Wake Custom is hindered by one step. The townsfolk's breathing has already slowed, and *The Room Exhales* happens once. If the PC fails, they stop him a moment too late and the song finishes anyway.


**If the party attacks the Mask-Maker,** use his stats from Section 2. He tries to talk first and fights only to protect the ritual. If he is defeated, the ritual is wrecked: the party can still sing the stanza and Winter still manifests, but with no keeper to steady the song, the body is unstable. *The Room Exhales* happens automatically every round, and every Wake Custom is hindered by one step.

**Read aloud as the ritual completes:**

> The last dancer settles, facing the effigy with all the rest. The Mask-Maker steps back to the edge of the floor, takes a breath, and sings, low and tired, the way the song is meant to be sung:
>
> *Well, you stripped the leaves from every tree,*
> *Left 'em bare as bone for the world to see...*
>
> And the room answers. Not one mouth moves behind its mask, but the refrain comes anyway, hundreds of voices, thin and far off, like a congregation heard through a church wall:
>
> *Please, old man, ain't you had enough,*
> *There's nothin' left in the holler to take from us.*
>
> Every frozen breath in the hall fogs at once and drifts toward the center of the floor. The effigy *breathes in*. The wire creaks. The burlap chest rises. The papier-mâché face turns on its neck, slowly, taking in every masked face that is looking at it, and where its painted eyes were there are eyes now, the color of the sky before snow.
>
> The baling wire parts with a sound like a fiddle string snapping. The coat rack clatters to the floor. And standing in the middle of the dance floor, where a papier-mâché effigy stood a moment ago, is a very old man.
>
> He is wearing the face of Old Man Winter: frost-bearded, crowned with the old bent nails, and something like a deep blue humor runs down from the pinholes of its eyes. He looks at you the way a man looks at somebody who's walked a long way to have a conversation he was hoping to avoid.

The Mask-Maker steps back into the shadow of the stage, bare-faced, and does not intervene again unless asked. He has done what he came to do. As he goes, he mutters the first hint: *"You'll have to fight a thing like that. But you sit up with it while you do."*

### One Last Word

Before the party's first move, give Old Man Winter a moment to speak. He is not interested in ambushing anyone, and a quiet exchange fits the adventure better than a silent charge. He knows exactly what the party is carrying.

**If the party carries any key:**

> "You found somebody's grief, or somebody's fire, or somebody's memory, and you mean to use it on me. I don't blame you for it. But I want you to understand what you're sending back out that door: the thaw, and the layoffs, and the children walking out down that road who don't come back. I was only ever asked to keep some of that a little further off. Was that so wrong of me?"

**If they carry none:**

> "Empty-handed. Well. That's honest, at least. But I want you to understand what you're asking for: the thaw, and the layoffs, and the children walking out down that road who don't come back. I was only ever asked to keep some of that a little further off. Was that so wrong of me?"

For a sharper callback, add a line for a path the party took:

- **Path A:** *"Ask the Reverend how many he'll bless down that road this spring. Every year this town bleeds its children out at the thaw. I only kept them home."*
- **Path B:** *"That fire down in the dark bought its keep same as I did. Difference is, it asked first."*
- **Path C:** *"She forgave you easier than she ought to have. Spring always does."*

Let a PC respond if they want to. The conversation ends when Winter decides it has. Then the fight begins.

### The Fight: Sitting Up with Winter

There is no talking Old Man Winter out of the body. The party has to bring him down. But he is a guest who has overstayed, and in these hills you see a guest off with the customs of a **wake**: you light the room, you say aloud what was lost, and you set a place at the table. On their turn, any PC can spend their action on one of these **Wake Customs** (below) instead of attacking. Each one stalls or weakens Winter, and a party that keeps them will find the fight far easier than one that only swings.

**Where the clues are.** Nobody explains the customs, but the party has already seen each one in the Hollow Season's sprites:

- **The church procession:** the preacher names each one who comes in. *The Telling.*
- **The miners' break:** carbide lamps kept burning low in the dark. *The Light.*
- **The boy and his plate:** food set out for one who isn't there. *The Table.*
- **Every sprite treats strangers as company:** greeted by name, offered a smoke, a plate set out. *The Guest's Courtesy.*

If asked, the Mask-Maker grumbles one hint per round, never an order: *"You light the room. You say what he took. You feed him, because you don't send a man off hungry. And you mind your manners the whole time. He's company."*

### Old Man Winter's Stats

```creature cypher
Old Man Winter, a Season Overstayed
Level 6
Health: 20 | Armor: 3 | Movement: Short

> **Frost-Sheathed.** Edged weapons and mundane fire barely register.
> **Kinship in Sorrow.** Tasks to resist his cold, despair, or influence are eased by one step for characters who showed genuine grief or loss in the scene.

- **Damage.** 6 points (a frost-rimed limb), OR 4 points of Intellect damage that ignores Armor (despair and cold clarity).
- **Snuff a Candle.** Instead of attacking, puts out one lit candle.

> **Frozen.** A PC reduced to 0 Health by him doesn't die. They freeze with the townsfolk and thaw when his body is burned.

---

**WAKE CUSTOMS:** Each costs a PC's action. Striking him in the same round spoils a Courtesy or the Table.
- **The Guest's Courtesy.** He takes no action on his next turn. Each courtesy works once.
- **The Light.** Each lit candle reduces his Armor by 1 (minimum 0).
- **The Telling.** Each success, from a different PC, reduces all his damage by 1 (maximum 2).
- **The Table.** He takes no actions for his next two turns. Works once.

---

**AT 0 HEALTH:** He does not die and is not sent away. He leaves his body behind, and it must be burned (see **The Burning**).
```

**Combat.** He rarely attacks to kill. He attacks to still, to slow, to convince. He does not so much walk as arrive. Make sure your players know the stakes are dire but not necessarily fatal. The horror is in the stillness, not the body count.

**Interaction.** He speaks rarely, and when he does, it is quiet, unhurried, almost kind. He recognizes kinship in sorrow, and it makes him, briefly, gentler. He genuinely believes he is doing Helvetia a favor. He may try to bargain directly, offering to spare someone specific or leave one part of the town untouched in exchange for the party leaving him be.

**GM Intrusions (Old Man Winter):**

- ***The Cold Remembers You:*** A PC recalls, vividly and against their will, the worst loss they've suffered to cold, illness, or time: a grandparent, a hard winter, a graveyard. Make it specific to the character (ask the player, or use their background). While affected, the character finds it hard to want to fight him.
- ***Frostbite of the Will:*** Instead of dealing damage, he simply asks a PC, gently, why they're bothering. The PC must succeed on an Intellect defense task or lose their next turn to a kind of grieving paralysis: not fear, just profound tiredness.
- ***The Room Exhales:*** Somewhere nearby, one of the frozen townsfolk's shallow breathing slows further, and somewhere in the Hollow Season, a lost soul flickers dimmer. Let the players feel the clock ticking on real people's lives without making it purely mechanical.

### The Wake Customs

Every key makes one custom easier, but none is required: a party with no keys can do each one at its stated Difficulty. Even with a key, it takes a PC's action under Winter's attention. Describe each key's moment in detail, not just a roll.

#### The Guest's Courtesy

Winter was invited, and he is bound by it more than he'd like. At any point in the fight, a PC can spend an action treating him as the guest he is. Each is an Interaction task, Difficulty 3 (Difficulty 2 if a candle burns nearby):

- Take his coat, hands open and empty.
- Offer him a chair.
- Pour him a cup of whatever's left: cider, coffee gone to ice, water.
- Greet him properly: *"You've come a long way. Sit down. You're welcome in this house."*

On a success, he **takes no action on his next turn.** Each courtesy works only once; he won't accept the same one twice. If anyone strikes him in the same round, the courtesy is spoiled.

#### The Light

Four tallow stubs stand on the four corner tables, kept for when the lamp oil runs out. Matches and lanterns die in this room; Winter's cold swallows ordinary fire. The candles need a real flame:

- A PC holds a wick and speaks a genuinely warm memory aloud (Intellect-based Interaction task, Difficulty 4 per candle).
- The cast-iron stove in the corner can be coaxed alive once (Speed task, Difficulty 4), after which candles can be lit from it (Difficulty 2 each).
- **Key: the Everburning Ember (Path B).** A PC touches the coal to a candle. The wick catches, and then the rest do, one after another around the room, with a sound like a long indrawn breath. The frost hisses off the windows in sheets of steam. All four candles are lit, and Winter can't snuff them for the rest of the fight.

**Each lit candle reduces Winter's Armor by 1** (to a minimum of 0). Winter can spend his turn snuffing one candle instead of attacking. A PC who spends their action guarding a candle keeps it lit that round.

#### The Telling

At a wake, you say aloud who the dead was. Here, you say what Winter took. A PC speaks a real loss in their own words: a line from the song's litany, something from their own life, or something they saw in Helvetia tonight (Intellect-based Interaction task, Difficulty 4). Winter has to hear it, and he tends to turn on whoever is speaking. A PC who accepts *The Cold Remembers You* and speaks about that memory has the task eased by two steps (and earns the XP for the intrusion). **Each successful Telling, from a different PC, reduces all of Winter's damage by 1**, to a maximum of 2.

- **Key: the True Final Verse (Path A).** A PC sings (or speaks, if singing isn't their character's way) the true final verse all the way through (Interaction task, Difficulty 4). It is the plea the town never finished, and it counts as both Tellings at once: Winter's damage drops by 2. A PC who takes damage before finishing falters and must start over on their next turn. Let the other PCs spend their actions keeping candles lit and Winter's attention off the singer.

#### The Table

At a wake you feed the guest. The Ball's food is still out on the long tables: ramps, cornbread, cider, coffee gone to ice. A PC sets a chair at the head of the long table with a plate and a cup (Speed task, Difficulty 3), then offers it to him with open hands and nothing within reach to use as a weapon (Intellect or Might task, Difficulty 4). He does not eat. He accepts, and he sits: **Winter takes no actions for his next two turns.** If anyone strikes him while he's seated, he rises, and the Table can't be offered again. It works only once.

- **Key: the Sarvis Berry (Path C).** A PC sets the berry on the plate. It is warm, dark, and impossible, and the room smells of spring for the first time all night. Winter looks at it for a long moment, and then he sits. No roll is needed, and he stays seated for two turns even if he is struck. (A berry already spent on thawing or healing can't also be used here.)

### Bringing Him Down

At 0 Health he does not die. He sinks into a chair, the one at the head of the table if he has already sat there, and the cold stops moving. Go to **The Settling**.

### The Settling

Bringing him down does not send him away. He leaves his body behind, and the town stays frozen.

**Read aloud** (choose the line that fits how it ended):

> Old Man Winter lowers himself into the chair at the head of the table the way an old man does at the end of a long visit, slowly, one hand on the armrest. *(Or, if he never took his seat at the Table: he sags into the nearest chair, as though someone had cut his strings.)* The frost on his beard stops spreading. Then, between one breath and the next, he is not an old man at all.
>
> What sits in the chair is a figure of wire and burlap and unspun wool, the effigy's head tipped forward on its chest like a sleeper's. It is only paper again. But the cold has not left. It has only stopped moving. The townsfolk are still frozen, every masked face still turned toward the chair, and the fiddle is still held on its single note.

Nothing returns to normal until the body is burned. If the party leaves it where it sits, the frost creeps back across the floor, and *The Room Exhales* once more with every scene that passes.

### The Burning

Once Old Man Winter is subdued, the Mask-Maker urges the party to finish the ritual: carry what is left of him out to the pyre in the town square and burn it.

**Read aloud:**

> The figure lies on the pyre, almost as if it were embracing it. Slowly, as the licks of flame start to taste the nape of its neck, the effigy's head turns on its side, as if he were falling asleep.
>
> The fire is strange. It burns without crackling at all, with a pale yellow hue. The smoke rises straight up, and as it climbs, the ash touches the hanging flakes, and one speck at a time, they begin to fall.
>
> The gray folds in on itself like paper curling in a flame, and the cold goes out of the world in one long exhale, like a held breath finally let go. Color comes back first: the orange of the fire, the red of somebody's scarf. Then sound: a roar of voices, a dog barking, and the clattering of buckled shoes.
>
> You enter the real town square and see a man in a grinning devil mask look down at the cup in his hand like it betrayed him. "Lord. What was in that cider?" Somebody laughs, and then everybody's laughing. Odell scratches his head, mutters that he must've called the last dance drunk, and swears he'll quit next year, same as every year.
>
> Nobody looks at you like anything unusual happened. In the town's own memory of tonight, nothing did. But you know that the Mask-Maker is going to make a journal entry of his own tonight. Something that truly may only be remembered by you, him, and the giddy grin kissing a piece of poplar.

Let the players sit with that quiet dissonance. They know what almost happened here, and no one else ever will, unless they choose to tell it.
